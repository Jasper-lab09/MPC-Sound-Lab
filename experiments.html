A structured catalog of experiments exploring sound, mathematics, and signal processing. Each experiment is runnable in the browser using the Sound Visualizer's interactive tools.

§ 01 Live Experiments
EXP-01
Fourier Series Visualization
Switch source to Signal Generator. Set a 440 Hz sine wave — the spectrum shows one peak. Switch to square — odd harmonics appear. Switch to sawtooth — all harmonics. This visually demonstrates the Fourier series decomposition of periodic waveforms.
Live
EXP-02
FFT Resolution Trade-off
Switch between FFT 512 and 8192 while speaking or humming. At 512, the waveform updates rapidly but frequency bars are coarse. At 8192, harmonics become visible but the display feels sluggish. This demonstrates the uncertainty principle.
Live
EXP-03
Spectral Leakage & Windows
Generate a sine wave at a frequency that doesn't fall on a bin boundary. Enable the rectangular window overlay — you'll see energy spread into neighboring bins. Switch to Hann or Hamming — the peak is cleaner but slightly broader.
Live
EXP-04
Harmonic Structures
Generate a sawtooth wave at 220 Hz and enable the harmonic toggle. The analyzer labels harmonic peaks at 220, 440, 660, 880 Hz. Switch to square — only odd harmonics are confirmed.
Live
§ 02 Planned Experiments
EXP-05
Wave Superposition
Visualize how multiple sine waves of different frequencies and amplitudes combine to produce complex waveforms. Demonstrates the linearity of the Fourier transform and the constructive/destructive interference patterns.
Planned
EXP-06
Euclidean Rhythms
Generate and compare Euclidean rhythms — the mathematically optimal distribution of hits across steps. Listen to how E(3,8) produces the tresillo, E(5,8) the cinquillo, and how these patterns appear in world music.
Planned
EXP-07
Mathematical Music
Map mathematical sequences (Fibonacci, prime numbers, Pascal's triangle) to musical parameters (pitch, rhythm, dynamics). Listen to the structural qualities of pure mathematics expressed as sound.
Planned
EXP-08
Sound-to-Visual Translation
Classify incoming audio into categories (speech, music, noise, beat, silence) and render distinct visual patterns for each. Tests whether visual representation can convey sound information to Deaf users.
Planned
