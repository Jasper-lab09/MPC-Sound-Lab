02 Mathematical Foundation
Fourier Transform

The Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) decomposes a time-domain signal into its frequency components:

DFT Definition
X[k] = Σn=0N-1 x[n] · e-2πi·k·n/N

The FFT computes this in O(N log N). Bin k corresponds to frequency:

fk = k · fs / N

Important: bin spacing (fs/N) is the distance between adjacent FFT output bins. It is not the same as frequency resolution — the ability to distinguish two simultaneous tones depends on the window's main-lobe width (see Window Function Explorer).

Click any ℹ icon beside a parameter for detailed equations and live calculations.

Autocorrelation & Energy Flux

Pitch detection uses normalized autocorrelation:

r[τ] = (1/N) · Σ x[n] · x[n+τ] / r[0]

The first peak after zero-lag gives the fundamental period. Frequency = fs / τ.

Beat detection computes instantaneous spectral energy:

E(t) = (1/K) · Σ |X[k]|²

beat ↔ E(t) > C · Ēavg

BPM is derived from the median of recent inter-beat intervals.

Harmonic analysis searches for peaks at integer multiples of the fundamental (fh = h·f0). Only peaks that align with the harmonic series and exceed 5% of the fundamental amplitude are labeled.

03 Physical Foundation

Sound is a mechanical wave — pressure variations propagating through air at approximately 343 m/s (at 20°C). The human ear perceives pressure oscillations in the range of 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

Wave equation
∂²p/∂t² = c² · ∇²p

where p is acoustic pressure and c is the speed of sound.

Digital sampling
fNyquist = fs / 2

At 44.1 kHz sample rate, frequencies up to 22.05 kHz can be represented.

Spectral centroid
SC = Σ fk · |X[k]| / Σ |X[k]|

Weighted mean frequency — measures timbral "brightness".

RMS & dBFS
RMS = √((1/N) · Σ x[n]²)

dBFS = 20 · log10(RMS)

dBFS is digital amplitude relative to full scale (0 = max). It is not calibrated to sound pressure level (dB SPL). Converting requires a calibrated reference microphone.

Window functions taper the signal at the boundaries to reduce spectral leakage. There is a trade-off: lower sidelobes (less leakage) come at the cost of a wider main lobe (poorer frequency resolution). The AnalyserNode uses a Blackman window by default; the overlay feature lets you compare rectangular, Hann, and Hamming windows.

04 Experiments and Findings
01
FFT Size — Time-Frequency Trade-off
Switch between FFT 512 and 8192 while speaking or humming. At 512, the waveform updates rapidly but frequency bars are coarse (86 Hz spacing at 44.1 kHz). At 8192, harmonics become visible but the display feels sluggish (5.4 Hz updates). Use the Capture A/B buttons to compare. This demonstrates the uncertainty principle: Δt · Δf ≥ 1/(4π).
Try: hum at FFT 8192, capture A → switch to 512, capture B → compare
02
Signal Generator — Fourier Series Visualization
Switch source to Signal Generator. Set a 440 Hz sine wave — the spectrum shows one peak. Switch to square — odd harmonics appear (1320, 2200, 3080 Hz). Switch to sawtooth — all harmonics (880, 1320, 1760 Hz). This visually demonstrates the Fourier series decomposition of periodic waveforms.
Try: sine → square → sawtooth at same frequency, watch harmonics appear
03
Window Function — Spectral Leakage Comparison
Generate a sine wave at a frequency that doesn't fall on a bin boundary. Enable the rectangular window overlay — you'll see energy spread into neighboring bins (leakage). Switch to Hann or Hamming overlay — the peak is cleaner but slightly broader. Compare bin spacing vs. actual resolution in the readout panel.
Try: 440 Hz sine, rectangular overlay → Hann overlay → compare leakage
04
Harmonic Analyzer — Harmonic Series Detection
Generate a sawtooth wave at 220 Hz and enable the harmonic toggle. The analyzer labels harmonic peaks at 220, 440, 660, 880 Hz (integer multiples). Switch to square — only odd harmonics are confirmed. The reliability indicator shows confidence based on harmonic alignment.
Try: sawtooth 220 Hz → square 220 Hz → compare harmonic patterns
05
dBFS — Digital Amplitude vs Perceived Loudness
Using the microphone, speak at different volumes. The RMS readout shows dBFS — digital amplitude relative to full scale, not calibrated SPL. A 6 dB increase roughly doubles amplitude but does not double perceived loudness (that requires ~10 dB). Adjust gain to see clipping at 0 dBFS.
Try: speak softly vs loudly → observe ~6-10 dB difference
